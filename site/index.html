SoilSight

Estimates a soil sample's gravel / sand / fines fractions from lab tray photos, with a calibrated range, a review flag when it is unsure, the most similar lab-tested samples as evidence, and a grain-size heatmap. Below are the model's real outputs on 63 held-out test samples it never saw during training, next to their laboratory sieve results.

How it works

  1. Find the soil. The tray is masked out; 12 full-resolution crops (~30 mm each, ~40 µm per pixel) and an overview are taken from inside it.
  2. Describe it. Two frozen DINOv3 vision models (Meta) turn crops and overview into feature vectors. Nothing is fine-tuned.
  3. Estimate. A ridge regression trained on ~250 lab-tested samples predicts the three fractions; photos of one sample are averaged.
  4. Say how sure it is. A conformal range scaled by how much the 10 most similar lab samples disagree with each other. Wide ranges are flagged for a technician.
  5. Show the evidence. The five most similar lab-tested samples and their real sieve curves.
  6. Show where. The heatmap applies the same kind of linear model to every 16 px patch; patches average back to the photo's estimate.

Model comparison

5-fold cross-validation on 199 development samples. Mean absolute error in percentage points; lower is better.

Limits

  • Visual estimate only: no plasticity, so not an engineering soil classification. Lab tests are still needed.
  • Trained on Montréal-region soils photographed with one lab setup. Other cameras, distances or soils are outside what it has learned.
  • Boundaries follow the BNQ sieve series: gravel > 5 mm, fines < 0.08 mm.
